Author guidelines

Information for Authors

These guidelines apply to every ACRI journal. Follow them and use the template: your accepted manuscript is converted automatically into the published web article and PDF, so a well-formatted manuscript means faster publication and fewer proof corrections.

Overview

  • Submit through the ACRI Journals portal. One account works for every journal.
  • Submissions are in English, as Microsoft Word (.docx) files made from the ACRI Journals template.
  • Review is double-blind. Keep author names and identifying details out of the manuscript file and put them on the separate title page.
  • Every published article is open access and gets a DOI.

Template and files to upload

Download manuscript template (.docx) Title page template (.docx)

  1. Anonymised manuscript (.docx, required): title, abstract, keywords, main text, references, and tables placed at the point of first mention. Figures are placed in the text at reduced size, with their captions.
  2. Title page (.docx, required): all authors with degrees, affiliations, ORCID iDs, the corresponding author's email, and the declarations.
  3. Figure files (required if you have figures): one file per figure, named Figure1.png, Figure2.jpg and so on. PNG, JPG or TIFF, at least 300 dpi at print size (at least 1600 px wide). Charts can also be SVG or PDF.
  4. Supplementary material (optional): appendices, questionnaires or datasets, as PDF, XLSX, CSV or ZIP, up to 25 MB each.
  5. Cover letter (optional).

Manuscript structure

Use the Word styles built into the template. The converter reads these styles, so do not format headings by hand.

  • Title (style Title): specific and informative, under 25 words. An optional subtitle goes on the next line in style Subtitle.
  • Key Points (Original Research only; style Heading 1 "Key Points"): three short paragraphs starting "Question:", "Findings:" and "Meaning:", 100 words in total.
  • Abstract (Heading 1 "Abstract"): for a structured abstract, start each paragraph with its label in bold. The labels are Importance, Objective, Design, Setting, and Participants, Main Outcomes and Measures, Results and Conclusions and Relevance. Reviews use Importance, Observations and Conclusions and Relevance.
  • Keywords: one paragraph starting "Keywords:", 4–6 terms separated by semicolons.
  • Main text: Heading 1 for sections (Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusions), Heading 2 and Heading 3 for subsections. Do not number headings.
  • Acknowledgments (Heading 1, title page only during review).
  • References (Heading 1 "References"): a numbered list, one reference per paragraph.

Style

  • Lora or Times New Roman 12 pt, 1.5 line spacing, A4, line numbers on. The template sets all of this.
  • Use British or American spelling, but consistently. Define each abbreviation at first use.
  • Report statistics with exact P values (P = .03; P < .001), effect sizes and 95% confidence intervals.
  • Write equations with Word's equation editor, not as images. They are converted to MathML.
  • Use footnotes sparingly. They are converted to endnotes.

Figures

  • Number figures in the order they are cited (Figure 1, Figure 2). Cite every figure in the text.
  • In the manuscript, insert each figure as an image on its own line, followed by a caption paragraph that starts "Figure 1." with a short title, then a description that explains every abbreviation.
  • Also upload each figure as a separate high-resolution file. The editorial office uses these for the published version.
  • Use clear sans-serif labels, at least 8 pt at final size. Avoid colour combinations that colour-blind readers cannot tell apart.
  • You must hold permission for any figure that is not your own. State the source and licence in the caption.

Tables

  • Build tables with Word's table tool. Never paste them as images.
  • Put a caption paragraph above each table, starting "Table 1." with a title. Put footnotes in a paragraph directly below, starting "Note." or with superscript letters.
  • One value per cell, with no merged cells in the body of the table. Give units in the column headings.

References

ACRI Journals uses AMA style (11th edition): references are numbered in the order they are cited, with superscript numbers in the text.1,2 List up to 6 authors, then "et al". Include the DOI wherever one exists.

1. Shah FP, Mehta YR. Institutional design for student-led research journals. Int J Educ Learn Futures. 2026;1(1):e2026001. doi:10.5281/zenodo.0000000

Reference managers: choose the "American Medical Association 11th edition" style in Zotero, Mendeley or EndNote.

Declarations (on the title page)

  • Author contributions, using the CRediT roles.
  • Conflict of interest disclosures for every author. Write "None reported" if there are none.
  • Funding, with the funder and grant number, and the funder's role.
  • Ethics approval and informed consent, with the approving body and reference number, where applicable.
  • Data sharing statement.
  • Use of AI tools (see the AI policy).

After you submit

  1. Technical check by the editorial office (about 5 working days). Incomplete submissions are returned to you.
  2. Editor screening for scope and quality. Some manuscripts are declined without review.
  3. Peer review by at least two reviewers. The first decision usually comes within 6–8 weeks.
  4. Revision: upload a revised manuscript and a point-by-point response to the reviewers.
  5. Acceptance: your DOI is reserved and you upload the final, non-anonymised files.
  6. Proofs: you check the web and PDF proofs in the portal within 3 days.
  7. Publication online under New Online, then in the next issue.

You can track every stage in the author portal and will get an email at each step.
